Chemistry without the brochure

Soil leaves. Clearcoat stays.

Maintenance detailing is applied surface science: surfactants, mechanical shear, dwell time, and thin films. Not magic in a bottle.

Two-bucket wash

Dirt is abrasive silica and fallout. A wash mitt picks it up. The rinse bucket drops grit off the mitt so the next pass does not drag it across clearcoat.

Gold Class is a mild anionic surfactant mix. At a light dilution it lowers water’s surface tension so oils emulsify and rinse. Stronger is not cleaner — it just leaves more residue and can dull already-thin wax.

soil + surfactant + water + shear → micelles in runoff

Why we keep panels wet

Evaporation concentrates leftover surfactant and minerals. That is water-spot chemistry: Ca²⁺ / Mg²⁺ salts crystallize on a warm panel. A wet hose and a cooler time of day keep the surface below the point where droplets pin and bake. Shade helps; it is not a gate.

Baggie test + clay

Bonded contaminants sit proud of the clearcoat. A plastic bag over a dry panel amplifies tactile feedback. Smooth = skip clay. Gritty = clay with Quik Detailer as a high-lubricity film so the bar shears dirt instead of the paint.

Clay does not “pull iron out of the panel.” It knocks off what is sitting on top. Over-claying micro-marring is why this is extra, not default.

Quik Wax is a thin sacrificial film

A spray wax lays down a short-chain polymer / carnauba-lite film. Thin is the point: enough to drop surface energy so water sheets and light dirt releases next wash. Thick paste does not last proportionally longer on a daily driver — it just smears and dust-magnets.

Leather is skin-adjacent collagen

Cleaner lifts body oils and dressing buildup. Conditioner replaces plasticizer that UV and heat drive out of the finish coat. Flooding leather drives water into stitching and foam. Product on the towel, thin film, wipe extra.

Why we do not wet-clean carpet

Car carpet is a thin pile glued to foam. A vacuum pulls loose grit off the fiber tips — that is the only layer a $50 interior can honestly reset. Wash soap misted on yarn leaves residue and pushes water into the pad. Damp foam is how mildew starts. Removable mats are different: they come out, get hosed, and can dry in open air. Put them back only when both faces are dry. Stains and odor in the padding are out of scope.
